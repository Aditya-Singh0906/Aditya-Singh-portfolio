Most teams do not need distributed tracing on day one. They need three things: dashboards they trust, alerts that mean something, and runbooks written before the incident.

## Start with the four golden signals

Latency, traffic, errors, saturation. That is it. If you cannot see those four on a single dashboard per service, add them before you do anything else.

## A CloudWatch baseline that pays for itself

```bash
aws cloudwatch put-metric-alarm \
  --alarm-name high-cpu-web \
  --metric-name CPUUtilization \
  --namespace AWS/EC2 \
  --statistic Average \
  --period 60 --evaluation-periods 5 \
  --threshold 80 --comparison-operator GreaterThanThreshold \
  --alarm-actions arn:aws:sns:ap-south-1:xxxx:pager
```

Multi-metric alarms and evaluation periods matter more than tighter thresholds — they kill flappy alerts without hiding real issues.

## Runbooks are documentation you actually reach for

Every alarm links to a page that answers three questions:

1. What does this alarm mean in plain language?
2. What is the first check to run?
3. Who owns the fix if it is not obvious?

The day you stop paging humans for problems that could self-heal is the day observability starts paying compound interest.
